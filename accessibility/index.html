What's tested, and where it falls short.

Everything below is something a test or a gate on this site already checks, with the counts read from the last run rather than typed, and the exceptions named rather than left for you to find.

  • How far it goes

    This site is tested against WCAG 2.2 at level AA on every route, and it's partially compliant, because of the exceptions listed below under what isn't right yet. The contrast gate holds 558 colour pairings before every deploy. Of those, 455 are text held at 7:1, the AAA level. Another 58 are held at 4.5:1, the AA level. The last 45 are controls and marks, held at 3:1. Nobody independent has audited it, so read this as what's tested and how, not as a certificate.

  • What's tested, every time

    66 accessibility tests run axe against the WCAG 2.0, 2.1 and 2.2 A and AA rules on every route, at phone, tablet and desktop widths, and report the AAA rules separately rather than leaving them out. Other tests hold what axe can't see: nothing scrolls sideways from 280 pixels wide, which is stricter than 400% zoom asks for, text survives a reader's own letter, word and line spacing, focus is never entirely hidden behind the navigation, and the navigation's controls stay at 44 pixels or more.

  • The settings you change yourself

    Forced colours, which is what Windows High Contrast uses, reduced motion and a request for more contrast are all switched on in the tests, on one page of every kind the site has. In forced colours the violation chart has to keep its bars and focus has to stay visible. With reduced motion nothing is allowed to move on its own. Asking for more contrast firms up the hairlines. The parts that differ between browsers are checked in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit, which is Safari's engine.

  • What isn't right yet

    The inclusive.io case study describes the first version of that app as the before. When I pointed axe at it on 24 September 2026 it found nine problems, one of them critical. That version's deployment is offline now, so it isn't embedded on this site and you can't repeat the count yourself. The now-playing strip cuts a long track title to one line, though the strip links to the track, where the whole title is. Tap targets are held at 44 pixels in the navigation, not yet on every link on every page. And the app embedded in that case study draws its own focus rings inside its own frame, where this site's tests can't see.

  • If something doesn't work for you

    Email craig@craighawkes.dev with the page and what happened. I'll answer today, the same as the home page says, and anything you report goes on this page until it's fixed.

  • When it was last checked

    Prepared on 24 September 2026. The whole suite last passed on 26 September 2026, all 551 tests, and the gates that hold contrast run before every deploy, so every number on this page is read from those runs rather than typed.
