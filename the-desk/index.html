the portrait screen, Quoin

Quoin · open source · the benchmark, printed

Print's had baseline grids since metal type. The web still hasn't got one.

So I built Quoin to measure it. My old site came last of nine, at ninety distinct drift values, and I published that. Then I fixed it: ninety down to fourteen. Then I swapped that design for these two and measured again, the same way: 45 and 60, behind every design system on the list. That wasn't good enough, so both now seat themselves on the grid with Quoin when they load, and the list below is the result. The design systems are measured as they ship, in my own October run.

the audit, accessibility

Accessibility statement · checked, not claimed

What's tested, every time

  • Tested against WCAG 2.2 AA on every route, at phone, tablet and desktop widths
  • 455 text colour pairings held at 7:1, the AAA level, by a gate that runs before every deploy
  • 66 accessibility tests, with the AAA rules reported rather than left out
  • Nothing scrolls sideways from 280 pixels wide
  • Forced colours, reduced motion and more contrast, all switched on in the tests

Partially compliant: one exception, on purpose, and it's published.

Three things most sites don't do

  1. OKLCH colour, gated. On 1.89% of sites, and the build fails if it gets flattened to sRGB.
  2. An llms.txt. On 5.61% of the top ten thousand. Nobody can show it does anything, so it stays because it costs a few kilobytes.
  3. Three separate builds. Phone, tablet and desktop each get their own component tree, not one squeezed three ways.
