Before · BPO Collections, 2025

It used to take four screens to pay a debt.

Find your account. Pick an amount. Type your card in. Check it, confirm it, then a 3D Secure redirect on top. Every one of those screens is a place where somebody already in arrears puts the phone down and decides it can wait.

Under it sat 299 VB files, 101 Web Forms pages, 131 SQL scripts and more than 97 stored procedures on a PCI‑DSS SQL Server. About six developers had tried moving it from SagePay to Fiserv. None of those attempts ever reached testing.

Six people had tried it. They asked me. I said yes.

After · from launch

Mobile payments up +80%

Mobile payments, from the day it launched.

For every ten people who used to pay on their phone, eighteen do now, and seven in ten of them pay with Apple Pay, because the button is right there on the one screen and it takes a thumb. 3D Secure, Google Pay, Open Banking, Direct Debit and bank validation all live in that same flow.

When my work is great, I'll tell you that it's fucking great, because I'm good at my job.

Under the button

I did it solo. 2,046 lines of C#, and not one failed cutover.

A library with 8 gateway operations and 20 request and response types, with every request signed using HMAC‑SHA256. The retries are idempotent: the client request ID means a timeout can't charge somebody twice. That's what you felt when you pressed pay twice up top.

Zero failed cutovers matters more to me than the 80, because of who is on the other end of it:

when it breaks, somebody already in arrears can't pay, and they'll assume that's on them.

I built all 117 components on my own, then rebuilt the lot for V2, because I couldn't leave it alone. 26 ATS workflows and 174 routes verified, axe finds 0 violations in both themes and the rest is for a person to check, on a three‑tier OKLCH token system from 280px to 5K with 360 contrast pairings checked on every run. Quoin · open source

Print's had baseline grids since metal type. The web still hasn't got one.

Quoin puts a web page on a baseline grid, and it measures how far a page drifts off one. I pointed it at my old site next to eight design systems, and it came last of the nine, at ninety distinct drift values, three times worse than the next worst. I published that. Then I got it from ninety down to fourteen, 9th to 4th. Then I swapped that design for this one and My Desk, measured again the same way, and they came out at 45 and 60, behind all eight. That wasn't acceptable, so both now seat themselves on the grid with Quoin when they load. This page drifts 6 ways at desktop widths, My Desk on a phone 4, and the design systems are measured as they ship.

fuck ups do happen, we all make mistakes, and trying to pretend we never do is just lying through our teeth.
