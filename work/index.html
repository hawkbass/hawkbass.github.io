How I'd run your team

Seven things you can check before you hire me.

What I'd bring to a department, written down only where it has already been done somewhere on this site you can go and look at.

  • A standard is a gate, not a statement

    A statement costs an afternoon and a gate costs you the argument, in public, the day it goes red, which is exactly why it works. I held my own AAA claim to one and it took the claim straight off me. Anything an agent writes is held to the same gates as any other contributor, and nothing merges unread.

    The gate that took my own claim off me

  • You get the number without asking twice

    My own work comes with its defects written down: the nine problems my verification found on inclusive.io, and the time my own site came last in my own benchmark. If you're hiring at this level, the useful question isn't whether somebody has open defects, it's whether they can tell you what they are without being asked twice.

    What I found wrong, and what I did about it

  • Own the number, not a share of it

    Owning a revenue number is different from contributing to one. As Head of Web the funnel breaking was my problem at nine on a Sunday, and every argument about page speed, form length and one more tracking script landed on my desk. Most technical decisions turn out to be staffing decisions in a costume.

    Head of Web, Paul McFadden Wealth

  • Hold both halves

    Almost every expensive problem I get called into lives in the gap between design and engineering. Design says it's an engineering problem, engineering says it's a design one, and it sits there for a year with nobody's name against it. I can hold both halves, which is the only reason I can see it at all.

    Four screens to one, and 80% more mobile payments

  • Finish what other people left

    Roughly half a dozen developers had a go at the SagePay to Fiserv migration and not one of them took it as far as testing. I finished it on my own, against a PCI-DSS estate that never stopped taking live money, with zero failed cutovers.

    The payments case study

  • Release like it's going to press

    Six years in print is six years of releases you can't take back: one shot, it goes on a press, and a wrong bleed arrives as a room full of boxes. I carried that into a payment migration where a bad release isn't an ugly page, it's somebody already in arrears who can't get the payment through.

    Six years at NOVAVAPES

  • Bring people on, and write it down

    I recruited an apprentice and brought them from junior to running the social channel outright, and as Head of Web I hired three of the team and co-managed seven across video, design and social. inclusive.io has 47 supporting documents and a 14-document handover pack, for a project with nobody to hand it over to, which is either thorough or daft depending on the morning you ask me.

    The handover, in the inclusive.io case study
